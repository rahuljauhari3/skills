# Credibility checks

This category judges whether the experience section reads as real, specific, and impressive — versus generic filler that could describe anyone. A scanner checks the mechanical stuff (bullet count); a good human-mimicking review also checks the substance, since that's what actually determines whether the resume gets a callback.

## 1. Bullet count per role
Each experience entry should have between 2 and 6 bullets. Fewer than 2 reads as thin; more than 6 buries the important points and usually means several bullets should be merged. Flag any role outside this range and suggest specifically which bullets to merge (pick genuinely related ones — e.g., two bullets about the same optimization theme — not just the first two).

## 2. Front-loaded impact
A strong bullet leads with the outcome, not the mechanism: "Increased X by 20% by doing Y" reads better to both a human and most scanning heuristics than "Did Y, which increased X by 20%." Flag bullets that bury a quantified result mid-sentence or at the end, and suggest a reordered version.

## 3. Quantification
Bullets with a number (%, count, time saved, dollar amount) are stronger evidence than unquantified claims. Not every bullet needs one, but a role with zero quantified bullets is a flag. Don't invent numbers that aren't in the source material — flag the gap and suggest the user add a real metric if they have one, rather than fabricating a plausible-sounding percentage.

## 4. Parallel structure and tense
All bullets within a role should start with a past-tense action verb (for past roles) or present-tense (only for a current, ongoing role), and should follow a consistent pattern: action verb + scope + tool/tech + measurable result. Flag inconsistent tense across bullets in a single role, and inconsistent punctuation (e.g., some bullets end with periods and others don't).

## 5. Specificity vs. genericness
Flag bullets that could describe almost any candidate ("Responsible for improving team processes") versus ones that name the actual tool, system, or outcome. This is the qualitative core of the review — call out the 1-2 weakest bullets specifically and show a rewritten version grounded in what the resume already states elsewhere (never invent new facts about the person's work).

## 6. Role-level summary (nice-to-have, not a hard fail)
A one-line summary under a job title framing the role's mandate can help, but its absence shouldn't drop a category score on its own — treat it as a suggestion, not a check.
